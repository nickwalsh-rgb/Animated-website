A hole in a jumper is a small job that gets bigger the longer you leave it. Darning is weaving a patch into the knit with a needle. It takes twenty minutes and no particular skill.

What you need

  • A darning needle: blunt, with a large eye
  • Yarn of the same weight as the jumper, in a matching colour (or a contrasting one, if you'd like the mend to show)
  • Something smooth and rounded to work over: a darning mushroom, an orange or a jam jar

How to do it

  1. Turn it inside out

    Slide the mushroom under the hole and pull the knit smooth over it without stretching.

  2. Stitch a frame

    Starting a centimetre outside the hole, where the knit is sound, sew a line of small running stitches around it. Leave a tail of yarn; no knots.

  3. Lay the uprights

    Sew parallel lines of yarn across the hole, close together, catching a stitch of sound knit at each end.

  4. Weave across

    Turn your work and weave over and under the uprights, alternating on each row, until the hole is filled.

  5. Finish

    Weave both tails into the back of the knit for a few centimetres and trim.
