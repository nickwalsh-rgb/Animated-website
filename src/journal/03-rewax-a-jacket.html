Waxed cotton is one of the few fabrics you can make waterproof again yourself. A jacket that's rewaxed when it needs it will outlast most things in your wardrobe.

When it needs doing

Look for dry, pale patches where the cloth creases: the elbows, shoulders and along the seams. If rain soaks in there and darkens the fabric where it used to bead and run off, it's time. For a jacket worn most days through winter, that's usually once a year.

What you need

  • A tin of wax dressing for waxed cotton
  • A bowl of hot water to stand the tin in
  • A lint-free cloth or sponge
  • A hairdryer

How to do it

  1. Clean it cold

    Brush off dry dirt, then wipe the jacket down with a sponge and cold water. No soap, no hot water and never the washing machine: all three remove the wax.

  2. Soften the wax

    Stand the open tin in hot water until the wax turns liquid, about twenty minutes.

  3. Work it in

    Lay the jacket flat. Rub the wax in with the cloth in small circles, a panel at a time, paying attention to seams, creases and pocket edges. Keep it off the corduroy collar and the lining.

  4. Even it out

    Go over the jacket with a hairdryer so the wax sinks in and any thick patches level off.

  5. Leave it overnight

    Hang it somewhere warm, away from other clothes, and let it cure before you wear it.
