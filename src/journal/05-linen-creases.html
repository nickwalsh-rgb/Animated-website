Linen is made from flax, a fibre with almost no stretch. Bend it and it stays bent. That's the crease, and it's the same quality that makes linen feel cool and dry when everything else is sticking to you.

Why we use it

Linen takes up moisture and lets it go again quickly, so it dries on you in warm weather. It's strong, stronger wet than dry, and it softens with every wash without wearing thin. A linen shirt at five years old is better than it was new.

Living with the creases

A pressed linen shirt stays pressed for about ten minutes, so we'd suggest not trying. Creases that come from wearing look right; the ones to avoid are the sharp folds from being left in a heap.

  • Take it out of the machine promptly, shake it hard and hang it to dry on a hanger.
  • Smooth the collar, placket and cuffs flat with your hands while it's damp.
  • If you do iron, iron it damp, on the reverse.

Washing

Wash at 30 or 40°C with similar colours and skip the fabric softener, which coats the fibre and makes it less absorbent. Line dry where you can: tumble drying is hard on flax and sets creases in.
