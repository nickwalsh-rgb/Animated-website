The best thing you can do for a wool jumper is wash it less. Wool resists smells and sheds dirt in a way cotton doesn't, so most of the time it needs air, not water.

Air it first

After wearing, hang your knitwear somewhere with moving air for a night: over a chair by an open window is enough. Most smells will be gone by morning. Brush off dried mud with a soft clothes brush once it has dried completely.

When it does need a wash

  1. Cool water, wool soap

    Fill a basin with cool water, 30°C at most, and a small amount of wool detergent. Ordinary detergent strips the fibre.

  2. Soak, don't scrub

    Press the jumper under the water and leave it for ten minutes. Rubbing and wringing are what cause felting.

  3. Rinse at the same temperature

    A sudden change from warm to cold shrinks wool. Keep the rinse water as cool as the wash.

  4. Press the water out

    Lift it out supported from underneath, then roll it in a towel and press. Never twist.

If the care label says machine washable, use the wool cycle with a low spin, and wash it inside out.

Dry it flat

Lay the jumper on a dry towel, ease it back into shape with your hands and leave it away from radiators and direct sun. Hanging wet wool stretches it; tumble drying shrinks it.

Pilling and storage

Small bobbles in the first few wears are loose fibres working their way out, and are normal. Remove them with a wool comb and they come back less each time.

Store knitwear folded, never on a hanger, and put it away clean: moths go for wool that has been worn. Cedar or lavender in the drawer helps.
