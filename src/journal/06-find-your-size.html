Because every style stays in the range in the same cut, working out your size is a job you do once. The most reliable way isn't to measure yourself. It's to measure something you already own and like the fit of.

Measure a garment, not a body

Lay it flat on a table, buttoned or zipped, and smooth it out without stretching.

  1. Chest

    Straight across from armpit to armpit. Double it to compare with a chest measurement.

  2. Length

    From the highest point of the shoulder, beside the collar, straight down to the hem.

  3. Sleeve

    From the shoulder seam down the outside of the arm to the end of the cuff.

  4. Waist and leg

    For trousers: straight across the top of the waistband, doubled, and the inside leg from crotch seam to hem.

Compare, then read the fit note

Match your numbers against the size guide. Then check the fit note on the product page: it tells you whether a style is cut close, regular or relaxed, and where it sits on the body. If you're between two sizes, take the smaller for knitwear and jersey, which give with wear, and the larger for outerwear you'll layer under.

If it isn't right

Send it back. Returns and exchanges are free for 30 days, and because every size is kept in stock, the one you need will be there.
